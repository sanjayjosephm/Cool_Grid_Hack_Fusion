# Elevating CoolGrid after AURA

**Analysis date: 3 October 2026, Australia/Sydney.**

This revises the project direction and compares it with the earlier alternatives against the actual judging criteria. It does not claim that the elevated application has been built, that a council has adopted it, or that its effects have been measured. The final ruling follows independent openings, rebuttals, a separate judging review and a source/output audit.

## Recommendation

Keep CoolGrid, and elevate it into a **regional service-resilience planning tool**. Its core question becomes:

> Which preparedness arrangement should our planning team verify or exercise next, given how heat, power failure and flood-related access loss affect our existing facilities and shared resources?

The first complete demonstration should help a municipal emergency/relief planning officer compare a proposed facility arrangement with a contingency and prepare a review brief. Asset managers supply building and resource facts; municipal committees review the plan; regional coordination addresses cross-boundary dependencies. A later investment view can compare professionally assessed upgrade packages under a shared budget.

The product ambition is wider than the original heat-and-home-electrification map: multiple hazards, service dependencies, regional coordination and a route to resilient, lower-energy public facilities. The demonstration must show a complete planning decision within that ambition. A larger map and more hazard layers do not establish greater usefulness.

## What the AURA experience changes

The user reports that the team’s similar AURA concept reached a previous hackathon final but not the top five. Council panellists reportedly questioned its Melbourne heat proposition, suggested regional or state applicability, and expressed stronger interest in flooding.

That is a valuable historical stakeholder signal. It challenges the original buyer hypothesis and requires a different problem statement. It is not a representative survey, a transcript, proof that every Melbourne resident is adequately served, or a diagnosis of every reason the entry lost. No fresh consultation or operational records were obtained for this analysis.

| Reported feedback or interpretation | Change to CoolGrid | What would repeat the mistake |
|---|---|---|
| Panellists reportedly noted existing Melbourne cooling mechanisms | Start with existing facilities, plans and services; ask which dependency or decision needs examination | Claiming councils have no cooling provision |
| Regional or state relevance might be stronger | Include isolation, shared resources and cross-boundary review in the product vision | Changing the geography dropdown without changing the decision |
| Flooding matters to some panellists | Give flooding its own service, access and facility constraints | Adding a flood score to heat and calling the sum a validated risk |
| User's takeaway: the problem statement overstated need | Use a documented failure mode and an attributable user decision | Returning to electrification-causes-deaths rhetoric |
| User's takeaway: the proposal needs more breadth and interest | Show competing arrangements and a specific next action, with ownership and uncertainty | Covering every disaster while leaving the action undefined |

## The decision domain exists, and so do serious incumbents

Regional areas are not empty territory. Greater Shepparton already publishes cooling locations and ordinary opening hours. Bendigo’s December 2025 dashboard combines warnings, road closures, outages and activated relief information. Those pages do not establish site capacity or dependable service in a particular emergency. [Shepparton heat information](https://greatershepparton.vic.gov.au/community/emergencies/heatwave), [Bendigo dashboard announcement](https://www.bendigo.vic.gov.au/about-us/news-and-media/city-launches-new-emergency-dashboard-help-residents-stay-safe).

CERA Online is already a modern all-hazards planning system. Its public guide describes mitigation owners, status, dates, consolidation and regional escalation. FloodZoom already provides flood analysts with forecasts, maps, gauges and agency intelligence. Their authenticated tools and actual council workflows were not inspected. Public descriptions cannot establish that a proposed calculation is absent. [CERA overview](https://www.ses.vic.gov.au/about-us/emergency-management-training/community-emergency-risk-assessment-cera), [CERA guide](https://www.ses.vic.gov.au/documents/8655930/9005355/CERA%2BUser%2BGuide%2B2.0%2BFeb%2B2023%2B.pdf/51fb446b-34ff-23b1-4eb9-b1f3b813c5ed?download=true&t=1676869554450&version=1.0), [FloodZoom](https://www.water.vic.gov.au/our-programs/floodplain-management/long-term-preparation-for-flooding).

Existing relief-centre guidance already covers capacity, continuity, isolation, access, essential resources and exercises. It distinguishes planning from activation and discourages publishing designated-centre lists that residents might mistake for available relief. CoolGrid should operationalise an existing review task and test its usefulness against competent practice. [LGV guidance](https://www.localgovernment.vic.gov.au/__data/assets/pdf_file/0027/226719/Emergency-Relief-Centre-Guidelines-v1.0.pdf), printed pages 9–11, 21 and 33.

Merri-bek provides a concrete asset-planning precedent: 24 council buildings were assessed in 2023–24 for physical vulnerability and continued service, including indoor comfort, stormwater and energy security. Its June 2026 case describes refurbishment prioritisation considering users, costs and risk appetite. This supports the relevance of an owned-building decision while limiting a broad novelty claim. It does not prove that CoolGrid would improve that programme. [Merri-bek case study](https://www.climatechange.vic.gov.au/__data/assets/pdf_file/0032/792734/case-study-merri-bek-city-council-retrofitting-buildings-for-climate-resilience.pdf).

A historical example gives the dependency story substance: Greater Shepparton’s flood recovery report records the October 2022 Causeway closure and resulting isolation and service-access impacts. It does not predict a future closure or validate this prototype. Use it to motivate a declared, fictional crossing-loss exercise. [Recovery report](https://greatershepparton.vic.gov.au/assets/files/documents/emergencies/flood/M25_34365_Greater_Shepparton_Recovery_and_Resilience_Plan_-_Progress_Report_-_June_2025.pdf), printed page 2.

## Applying the four judging criteria

The event uses **COP31 alignment 30%, build quality 30%, creativity 20% and presentation clarity 20%**. Ties favour alignment. These are the published criteria; the comparisons below are our evidence-qualified judgements, not official marks or winning probabilities.

- **Alignment:** show the intended action and its plausible climate contribution. A resilience subproblem is permitted; reaching every priority or quoting a global percentage is unnecessary.
- **Build quality:** show a complete functioning core or realistically testable mockup, with limitations and failures. Tiny reference calculations do not establish a finished product.
- **Creativity:** identify an inventive contribution against competent prior practice. An established mechanism can support a useful design; its novelty and comparative usefulness still require evidence.
- **Presentation:** explain one owner, decision and result within two minutes. A platform tour does not substitute for a clear problem and user.

## The proposed product contract

**Primary preparer:** municipal emergency/relief planning officer, working with asset and service managers. **Review:** municipal committee and relevant regional coordination. **Future programme use:** comparable council-authored evidence across a region and assessed investment options. Current Victorian material assigns state and regional relief/recovery coordination to EMV; do not copy obsolete administrative names into the app. [Current roles](https://www.vic.gov.au/relief-recovery-victoria), [planning responsibilities](https://www.ses.vic.gov.au/about-us/state-and-regional-emergency-plans).

The reviewer defines one service and operating window for each scenario. Cooling respite at a given hour and temporary flood shelter are different services; capacities cannot silently transfer between them. Demand means a declared planning requirement, not everyone counted in Census or a heat-vulnerability map.

**Inputs:** facility and physical-resource IDs; service-specific capacity; opening window; staffing; required utilities and backup configuration; authorisation/evidence status; declared access links; actions, costs and prerequisites; source, date and verifier. Real scenario requirements and preference/equity policies belong to the responsible planners.

**Processing:** apply each scenario’s explicit failures; exclude failed or unresolved prerequisites; reserve shared resources once; allocate only eligible capacity; report shortfalls by community/subgroup; compare proposed arrangements and action costs without mixing hazards into one reassuring score. Keep alternative scenarios distinct from claimed simultaneous-event probabilities.

**Outputs:** conditional service-allocation witness, remaining gaps, dependencies causing those gaps, scenario trade-offs, questions and assigned evidence owners, and the proposed next exercise or assessment. A future investment brief must identify assessed effects, costs/ranges, authorisation and sensitivity before presenting a funding preference.

A narrow calculation can proceed under declared assumptions, but overall service remains unverified whenever a required physical or operational fact is missing or outside the model. An allocation total must not produce a confirmed-service badge while those prerequisites remain unresolved.

**Required acceptance checks:** capacity/resource conservation; stable dependencies independent of display names; different service and time-window constraints; appropriate power and energy checks; unverified access blocked; unknowns preserved; no implicit transfer of capacity across scenarios; explicit per-community/subgroup shortfalls; alternatives and ties exposed; no activation, refuge-safety or evacuation-routing label.

## Worked reference exercise

The root agent newly authored a small Python reference exercise on 3 October. Every location, requirement, cost, action effect and capacity is fictional. Two zones each require four abstract service places. Three arrangements are checked against three separate stress scenarios:

| Arrangement | Illustrative action-cost units | Heat, grid available | Heat with outage | Flood with crossing unavailable |
|---|---:|---:|---:|---:|
| Existing central facility | 0 | 8 | 0 | 4 |
| Configure the central backup assumption | 3 | 8 | 8 | 4 |
| Make both local facilities available under stated assumptions | 4 | 8 | 8 | 8 |

These numbers are conditional allocations in a constructed example, not protected residents, verified facilities, upgrade effectiveness or monetary returns. Heat and flood figures do not add into a total impact. A competent worksheet can reproduce the same answer.

With only one crew available during the outage, the two local facilities can supply only four places. Equal aggregate totals can leave different zones without allocation. The output exposes those vectors and example facility/crew/allocation witnesses; it does not impose an equity policy.

The current reference passes 13 root-authored diagnostics. Independent reviewers found that the earlier crossing closure depended on a display name, an omitted prerequisite could raise a raw error, and aggregate results lacked an actionable witness. The reference now uses declared stable access-dependency IDs, reports example allocations, and treats omitted Boolean prerequisites as unknown blockers. Its initial empty-network error was also repaired.

The advocate's earlier failing rename record is preserved under the old source hash, with the repaired result separately recorded. The reference still lacks a complete model of time windows, shared generators, water, thermal conditions, subgroup requirements, attendance and actual operations. The product contract requires those checks or explicit unresolved evidence; this script does not establish the complete Continuity Lab application. Its finite allocation objective maximises entered places, not equitable access, avoided deaths, safety or expected loss.

The backup Boolean is a supplied premise; electrical power, usable energy, circuits and runtime are not derived by this script. Alternative allocation vectors are exposed across eligible facility subsets, but not every equally optimal allocation within a subset. Both limitations must survive the interface and pitch.

The failed crossing is encoded in scenario configuration, but the reference output does not yet name that failed link as the binding cause of a shortfall. A complete Continuity Lab explanation must trace the gap to that dependency and the next verification. The current script is therefore not a complete evidence-to-review flow.

The continuing researcher audited primary sources and handed findings to separate advocate and falsifier instances. Both produced openings before reading the opposing argument, then exchanged the complete records for a rebuttal round. A separate limited-source, nonexpert judging reviewer applies the four criteria; the root audits sources and observed outputs before the final ruling.

The agents are perspective-diverse instances of the same model family. Limited source access simulates part of nonexpert oversight; it does not establish that the judge is a weaker model. We did not measure cross-provider diversity, Elo persuasion, debate accuracy gains or internal reasoning traces. Public argument summaries, source limits and counterexamples govern this record.

**Final ruling: conditionally lead with C1 Continuity Lab**, use a C3-style evidence/assessment handoff inside that review, and retain C2 Investment Gate as the assessed capital-program pathway. This is an execution recommendation under the current evidence, not a score, winner prediction or finding of established buyer demand. If the complete review cannot be demonstrated, reduce the sites/options or label the narrower fallback honestly; do not call a map or calculator the completed regional platform.

The judge assessed 16 separate options: the three elevated variants, historical proposal, current app, generic dashboard and all ten alternatives. Each received distinct alignment, build, creativity and presentation assessments. The root retained the recommendation after checking the current app, official COP31 and Victorian role sources, available primary-source records/excerpts, final hashes and observed challenge results. **Surviving dissent:** the falsifier sees C2 as the more substantial long-term investment product and warns against a permanent exercise tool detached from that path. The advocate considers the C1 preparedness decision substantial in its own right. The ruling keeps the concrete sequence—dependency gap, qualified assessment, potential investment—without requiring a capital optimiser in the weekend demonstration. Creativity against competent existing practice remains unresolved.

FleetCharge and OffcutPlan remain strong backup directions with clearer bounded references; RecycledEvidence remains a smaller evidence-review option, and ApartmentFOGO/ClimateOdds retain conditional cases. None receives an automatic advantage from being smaller, broader, easier or preferred by the team. No direct head-to-head product comparison establishes superiority either way.

## Final refinements to carry into the proposed build

1. **Replace the buyer hypothesis.** Prepare an internal service review for a named council planning role; use regional dependencies as a substantive scenario, rather than a wider map setting.
2. **Keep the ambition and complete one action.** Demonstrate Continuity Lab from defined service to next verification/exercise brief. Treat Investment Gate as a separate assessed decision stage, with Retrofit Brief supporting missing evidence.
3. **Make scenarios and service units explicit.** Heat respite, outage operation and flood relief retain their own capacity, window and prerequisites. Do not add their totals or imply joint-event probabilities.
4. **Make evidence alter the result.** Source, date, verification state and stable dependency IDs belong in the flow. Missing required facts must lead to a question and owner, not a reassuring default.
5. **Expose resource and distribution trade-offs.** Count each constrained resource once; show the witness and per-community gaps. Preserve the staffing counterexample and declare the equity policy or its absence.
6. **Compare with competent practice.** Freeze the same-input worksheet and fresh test cases before claiming improved detection, effort or explanation. Treat arithmetic parity as a finding, not a reason to weaken the comparator.
7. **Replace unsupported product claims.** Remove assumed protection/emissions/readiness claims from the proposed new flow. Tie COP31 to resilience now; quantify energy intensity only with assessed or measured evidence and a stated denominator.
8. **Present a reviewable two-minute decision.** One officer, one service, competing arrangements, a binding dependency and the next action. Disclose fictional inputs, current implementation limits and material AI use.

## Implementation transition and claim limits

The current repository is still the original illustrative Melbourne neighbourhood application. It already normalises/clamps its score to 0–100; the historical raw-range error is not a current implementation defect. It still uses assumed intervention effects, emissions/coverage figures and electrification-ready recommendations, and does not implement this multi-hazard planning workflow.

The proposed rebuild changes the data contract from neighbourhood scores to facilities, service requirements, dependencies, evidence and actions; replaces readiness categories with conditional status and questions; and replaces assumed benefit percentages with explicitly entered assessed or illustrative changes. A map becomes context for a planning decision. Preserve sources and dates on inputs and label demonstration values where they appear.

Prior results remain historical evidence. There are no new participant results, current stakeholder endorsement, demand estimates, measured workflow gains, physical service outcomes or climate impacts. Placebo and expectancy remain unmeasured. The event permits testable mockups, so these limits should constrain the claims rather than falsely disqualify the project.
