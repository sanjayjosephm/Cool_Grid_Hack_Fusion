# CoolGrid — team discussion brief

**3 October 2026.** Read the [full elevation and judging analysis](04-coolgrid-elevation-and-judging.md) for sources, comparisons and debate findings. This is a proposed direction; the repository's existing app still demonstrates the earlier neighbourhood concept.

## The decision to discuss

Keep CoolGrid, but make it a **regional public-service resilience planning tool**. Lead with one complete preparedness decision; retain assessed building investment as the larger product pathway.

Proposed one-sentence description:

> CoolGrid helps council planning teams compare how their existing service arrangements hold up when heat, power failure or flood-related access loss disrupts facilities, and identify what to verify or exercise next.

The proposed initial user is a municipal emergency/relief planning officer working with asset and service managers. Regional coordination is an expansion path for shared dependencies. No council has endorsed this proposal.

## What AURA taught us

The reported council feedback challenged the Melbourne heat proposition and suggested flooding and regional relevance. Treat it as a positioning signal, rather than proof that heat is solved or that a wider dashboard will sell.

Existing cooling provision, emergency dashboards, flood intelligence, continuity guidance and council building-assessment programmes are substantial. Our pitch should start with those arrangements and show a decision they need to review. More map layers alone do not establish value.

The revised problem hypothesis is specific: **a service plan can depend on a crossing, crew, power supply or facility that becomes unavailable in the scenario being reviewed**. Whether our interface helps planners handle this better remains to be tested.

## Three connected product stages

| Stage | Decision | Recommendation |
|---|---|---|
| **Continuity Lab** | Which fallback arrangement should we verify or exercise next? | Lead the demonstration: a few facilities, explicit dependencies and separate scenarios |
| **Investment Gate** | Which professionally assessed upgrade package fits our budget and service objectives? | Next stage; needs credible costs, assessed effects and energy baselines |
| **Retrofit Brief** | Which missing site/electrical fact should an assessor confirm? | Supporting evidence module; fallback if the broader workflow cannot be completed |

Do not present the reference allocation script as this entire product. Time windows, actual electrical performance, water, thermal suitability, shared generators and equity policy remain outside that script.

## The demonstration story

Use fictional facilities and a schematic. Define the service, operating window and capacity unit separately for each scenario.

In the reference example, two communities each require four abstract places. The existing central arrangement allocates **8 / 0 / 4** across normal heat, heat with outage and a separate flood-crossing closure. Assuming central backup changes that to **8 / 8 / 4**. Assuming two local facilities are available changes it to **8 / 8 / 8**, at a higher fictional action cost.

Then reduce staffing to one crew. The local arrangement supplies only four places during the outage, while central backup can supply eight. This prevents a simplistic “local is always better” conclusion. Show the assumptions, per-community gaps and example allocation that produced the result.

These are constructed calculations. They do not measure protected residents, actual site readiness or upgrade effectiveness. A competent worksheet can reproduce them.

Independent reviewers reproduced the repaired reference arithmetic against **72 tiny cases spanning its declared scenarios** and **300 tiny normal-heat allocation cases**. The checks support narrow mechanics. They do not establish an elevated app, practical advantage or field reliability.

## How this answers the judging criteria

- **Alignment — 30%:** a specific climate-related service dependency, owned planning action and credible path to use.
- **Build quality — 30%:** a complete testable flow from entered evidence to comparison, gap, explanation and review brief. Current reference tests alone are insufficient.
- **Creativity — 20%:** potentially useful dependency explanations and comparisons. Existing planning practice is the strong comparator; originality remains uncertain.
- **Presentation — 20%:** one officer, one failure, competing arrangements and one next action within two minutes.

All ten earlier alternatives were reconsidered under all four criteria. FleetCharge and OffcutPlan remain practical backups; RecycledEvidence, ApartmentFOGO and ClimateOdds retain narrower conditional cases. None has demonstrated superior practical value. The team's commitment supports choosing CoolGrid for execution; it does not create a score advantage.

## Questions for the team

1. Can we demonstrate the complete Continuity Lab review flow, rather than just a calculation or map?
2. What exact service and operating window are we comparing, and which required facts remain unresolved?
3. What does the planner do after seeing the result: verify a dependency, assign an assessment, or schedule an exercise?
4. Can we explain what changes from competent current practice without claiming an untested benefit?

Split the remaining work around evidence and service definitions, the comparison/explanation flow, and the two-minute demonstration. Preserve unknowns and scenario differences throughout. Record AI and data use in the submission.

The discussion should end with a concrete scope decision: **Continuity Lab now, assessed investment next**, or a clearly labelled narrower fallback. Demand, adoption, usability, human expectancy/placebo effects and climate outcomes remain unverified.
