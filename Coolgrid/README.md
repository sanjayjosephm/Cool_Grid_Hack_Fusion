The latest recommendation keeps CoolGrid and changes its proposed user decision: a council planning team compares service arrangements across separate heat, outage and flood-access scenarios, then selects a dependency or arrangement to verify or exercise. An assessed public-building investment view is the next stage. Historical AURA panel feedback was reported by the user; no fresh consultation or operational records establish demand or comparative value.

The earlier finding remains: FleetCharge and OffcutPlan contain concrete bounded allocation mechanisms, while RecycledEvidence, ApartmentFOGO and ClimateOdds demonstrate supplied-field checks or content semantics. None has demonstrated robust incremental practical value over competent baselines. Exploratory stronger heuristics matched the showcased optimisation wins; this does not prove equivalence across all possible cases. The new CoolGrid reference likewise supports narrow arithmetic, not a superiority claim.

Stakeholder roles in the earlier validation were simulated. The reference benchmarks did not test finished applications, this repository's UI, real operational records or human participants. Learning, workload, adoption, psychological placebo effects and climate outcomes remain unverified. The 93 earlier adversarial diagnostics and the new reference challenge counts include confirmations of model limitations; they are not product success rates.

The original assessment was exported from its saved Page and checked against the matching Word document. The later files summarise the saved research, debate and validation records in Markdown. Their provenance sections distinguish source findings, proposed tests, executed diagnostics and superseded observations. The source JSON records and Python harnesses are not included in this Markdown-only export. The original repository application remains separate from the proposed elevation.
