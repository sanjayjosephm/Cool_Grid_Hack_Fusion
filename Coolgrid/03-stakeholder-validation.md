# Stakeholder debate and concept-level validation

**Research and adjudication date:** 2 October 2026, Australia/Sydney.

**Status:** Completed simulated stakeholder debate and diagnostic reference-code review. Human, operational and climate outcomes remain unverified.

This is the third part of the CoolGrid analysis record. Read the [original CoolGrid assessment](01-coolgrid-analysis.md) and [cross-priority research and expert debate](02-research-and-debate.md) for the earlier argument and candidate-selection history. ## Verdict

All five finalists contain a concrete, climate-relevant intermediate mechanism. The review does **not** establish a robust practical advantage over competent alternatives.

FleetCharge and OffcutPlan have substantial allocation logic, but reasonable additional simple baselines matched their showcased optimisation wins. RecycledEvidence and ApartmentFOGO matched competent spreadsheet or inspection checks. ClimateOdds has correct probability semantics and a proposed learning study; no learners received a lesson.

The surviving proposal is to build the smallest auditable demonstration, state its assumptions and compare it fairly. There is no evidence here of five finished applications, stakeholder endorsement, usability improvement, economic return, learning efficacy, physical accessibility, adoption or climate impact.

| Project | Climate priority | Final position | What survived |
|---|---|---|---|
| E1 FleetCharge | Electrification | Retain a bounded charging-plan demonstrator; retract general scheduling superiority. | Exact feasibility and schedule checks within an explicit discrete model. |
| G1 RecycledEvidence | Green Industrialization | Narrow to a clarification ledger; discard certification, ranking and informational-superiority claims. | Supplied-field arithmetic, scope and evidence-completeness checks. |
| G2 OffcutPlan | Green Industrialization | Retain a fixed-order cut-plan ledger; retract robust optimiser superiority. | Eligible-stock allocation, kerf conservation and catalog-bounded purchase requirements. |
| A2 ClimateOdds | Climate education | Retain correct content and a testable lesson proposal; discard efficacy and placebo verdicts. | Probability semantics and explicit fictional decision assumptions. |
| W2 ApartmentFOGO | Zero Waste & Methane Reduction | Retain a supplied-constraint mockup; hold the behavioural pitch. | Conditional rejection of entered access, permission and capacity blockers. |

Baseline parity does not prove that a project is worthless or a placebo. It removes demonstrated informational advantage in the tested cases. Effort, usability, trust and expectancy remain separate, unmeasured questions.

## Scope and event standard

The user confirmed **no real stakeholder contacts or operational records**. There were **zero human participants**. Reviewers did not invent consultations, respondents, testimonials or effect estimates.

The event accepts working proofs of concept and realistic, testable mockups. Labelled synthetic inputs can therefore support a submission demonstration. That permission does not convert a simulated result into a measured human or climate outcome.

The analysis retained the published weights: 30% alignment, 30% build quality, 20% creativity and 20% presentation. No competition score or winning probability was inferred from these diagnostics.

- **Alignment:** explain the actual user action and its climate-relevant causal chain.
- **Build quality:** show bounded, independently checked behaviour, including failure and unknown-input cases.
- **Creativity:** demonstrate an added decision or workflow benefit beyond competent prior practice.
- **Presentation:** expose assumptions, unresolved inputs, model resolution and incomplete search.

These are assessment principles, not proof that the five proposed products have been built.

## How the debate worked

Three separate LLM instances produced independent round-zero reviews. Their roles were simulated functional perspectives, not representatives of the named professions.

| Review | Perspectives represented | Main challenge |
|---|---|---|
| Government and audit | Regulator, public buyer, council finance, taxpayer, independent auditor | Authority, input truth, public value, equitable clarification and unjustified feasibility labels. |
| Industry and operations | Business owner, dispatcher, installer, workshop worker, building manager, incumbent tool | Who pays; who enters and updates data; hidden execution labour; competent alternatives. |
| Affected people and behavioural science | Residents, disability/access advocate, cleaners, collectors, teachers, learners | Burden transfer, exclusion, confidence calibration, learning transfer and expectancy. |

Round one exchanged those openings. Each reviewer accepted, revised or rejected particular claims and authored executable challenges against the reference logic. The root agent adjudicated the public arguments against outputs and the source audit.

The instances were perspective-diverse, not a heterogeneous panel of different model architectures. The adjudicator was not demonstrated to be a weaker model. There was no Elo training or optimisation for persuasive rhetoric. Agreement was not treated as validation: code-level counterexamples and stronger baselines changed the verdicts.

### What the independent openings agreed on

The causal chains were plausible, but incomplete. A valid calculation can support an intermediate action without proving the inputs are true, the action is authorised, or the downstream outcome occurs.

All three reviews demanded the same facts, constraints and objectives for candidate and baseline. They also challenged evaluations that count only solver or checking time while excluding transcription, inspection, maintenance, exceptions, physical handling, supplier response work or teacher preparation.

The roles raised different burdens:

- Dispatchers may receive another data-maintenance task, while drivers perform plug swaps or inherit missed duties.
- Procurement reviewers may save checking effort by transferring repeated evidence work to suppliers.
- Workshop workers may save stock while spending more time inspecting, retrieving and cutting scraps.
- Teachers may gain an attractive lesson but incur setup and marking work without better transfer.
- Residents may gain a shorter route while cleaners or collectors receive worse handling conditions.

These are hypotheses to test. No stakeholder here reported that the burdens actually occurred.

### What changed in rebuttal

The government reviewer accepted operator and people objections, but rejected the claim that unmeasured labour must outweigh a computational stock difference. Neither net benefit nor net loss had been observed.

The industry reviewer retained intermediate clarification as legitimate procurement work, while conceding that the matched spreadsheet already supplies the same checks. Stronger simple charging and cutting policies weakened the featured optimisation claims.

The people reviewer revised an earlier generic-output control proposal: removing information changes the task and cannot isolate expectancy. A valid component ablation, a competent active baseline and a fixed-output presentation comparison answer different questions.

All reviewers rejected using the absence of human evidence as an automatic event exclusion. They instead limited what the prototype could claim.

## E1 — FleetCharge

**Owner and action:** a council-depot dispatcher or charging planner chooses an overnight plan, or requests a scheduling/infrastructure change when the stated requirements cannot all be met.

**Causal chain:** reliable duty, energy, charger and residual-site-capacity inputs → valid plan or specific deficit → authorised operational change → potentially fewer charging-related missed duties. Removing an electrification barrier, buying vehicles and reducing emissions are later links.

Existing managed charging already covers power ceilings and arrival/departure scheduling. That establishes both the problem and a strong incumbent baseline; it does not validate this proposed software. [US Department of Energy guidance](https://www.energy.gov/cmei/femp/managed-ev-charging-federal-fleets)

| Simulated stakeholder | Damaging objection |
|---|---|
| Government/finance/taxpayer | Why fund another scheduler, and who verifies electrical headroom? Include data maintenance and failure recovery. |
| Dispatcher/installer/incumbent | Existing controls or a spreadsheet may suffice. Yesterday's duty and building-load inputs can make today's plan useless. |
| Drivers/affected citizens | Plug swapping, late arrivals and missed essential services cannot be hidden by a reassuring readiness badge. |
| Independent auditor | Heuristic failure is not infeasibility; coarse-model failure is not continuous electrical impossibility. |

The proposed payer is a fleet/operations budget, not an established customer. Dispatchers own duties while facilities staff must verify residual power; drivers may bear input or plug-swap work. A nominal headroom result could encourage adding vehicles and remove operational slack. These are incentive and ownership hypotheses. A later evaluation needs completed mandatory duties, measured site peaks, total planning/recovery minutes and driver interventions, rather than solver speed alone.

### Exact surviving evidence

The reference searches for a schedule satisfying **every mandatory vehicle** in an explicitly quantised model: integer time/energy units, connection windows, charger compatibility/exclusivity, rates, efficiency and residual site capacity.

| Diagnostic | Exact/reference | Original active baseline | Additional evidence |
|---|---:|---:|---|
| Restricted-charger tie | 2/2 ready | 2/2 ready | Myopic first-fit gets 1/2. The competent matching baseline already resolves this trap. |
| Future charger availability | 3/3 ready | EDF gets 2/3 | Myopic first-fit and industry's last-compatible-opportunity rule both get 3/3. |
| Ordinary eight-vehicle case | 8/8 ready | 8/8 ready | The other simple comparator also gets 8/8. |
| Tiny enumerated cases | 36/36 agreements | Not a population comparison | Evidence concerns finite model feasibility, not general practical value. |

The additional last-opportunity policy was developed **after observing the diagnostic case**. It is a reasonable falsification of the showcase, but not a predeclared held-out demonstration of general heuristic parity or dominance.

Two scope limits survived:

1. Government supplied two 0.4 kWh needs under a 1 kWh cap. A 1 kWh quantum rounds each need up and rejects the whole-fleet plan; a 0.1 kWh model accepts the 0.8 kWh total. People's separate 0.5 + 0.5 kWh example illustrates the same limitation.
2. On an impossible whole-fleet case, exact search returns no complete plan while a heuristic can ready one vehicle. Exact search did not optimise the largest served subset. Comparing those ready counts as though the objectives were identical would be unfair.

### Final refinements and kill gates

Show units, quantum, fixed efficiency and all-mandatory objective on every result. Separate invalid input, no whole-fleet plan, no heuristic plan and search-limit status. Do not silently relax departures or omit duties.

Freeze EDF, compatibility/scarcity and last-opportunity baselines before testing new varied cases. Preserve changed-departure and failed-charger cases. Any released schedule violating supplied constraints, favourable unknown default or real-world impossibility claim from coarse resolution requires repair or retraction.

**Allowed pitch:** “A small, auditable charging-plan simulator under stated assumptions.”

**Unverified:** real duties, continuous electrical feasibility, stale-data recovery, driver interventions, staff effort, avoided upgrades, commercial demand, fleet adoption and emissions.

## G1 — RecycledEvidence

**Owner and action:** a procurement officer reviews one nonstructural product category, decides which supplied declarations are internally comparable and approves specific clarification requests.

**Causal chain:** entered declarations and human-approved boundary → correctly identified inconsistency or unknown → precise clarification → better-informed comparison. Authentication and increased recycled-material purchases do not follow automatically.

The current procurement guide supports mass-based recycled content, separate product/packaging boundaries and caution about unsupported claims. It is also the basis of the competent spreadsheet comparator. Its example criteria are not universal legal thresholds. [DCCEEW Sustainable Procurement Guide 2024](https://www.dcceew.gov.au/sites/default/files/documents/sustainable-procurement-guide-2024.pdf)

| Simulated stakeholder | Damaging objection |
|---|---|
| Government buyer/auditor | The same guide already supplies the checks. A reference and correct arithmetic cannot certify a declaration. |
| Business owner/reviewer | Reading, interpreting, entering and reconciling documents may cost more than automated checking saves. |
| Small supplier | Repeated questions or rigid formats may penalise equivalent evidence and transfer work to suppliers. |
| Taxpayer/people advocate | Does the purchasing decision improve, or does paperwork merely look more authoritative? |

The proposed payer is the procurement function; willingness to pay is unknown. Suppliers could improve declaration presentation or change denominators without improving supplied goods. A later comparison should count necessary/correct questions, missed defects, false flags, total intake-to-review time and supplier rework, then trace whether a purchasing decision actually changed.

### Exact surviving evidence

Candidate and separately written spreadsheet reached the same ten core answer keys. Those keys were hand-authored, not a blinded procurement study. The suite covered valid declarations, zero/unknown denominators, product/packaging mixing, overcounted mass, mismatched percentages, missing evidence, overlapping categories, negative virgin mass and legitimate rounding.

Independent tests now reject nonfinite values, blank/non-string evidence and percentages outside 0–100. A consistent declaration remains unauthenticated.

Rounding also needed a different kind of question. Unrounded measurements of 4.76 kg recycled plus 5.26 kg virgin equal 10.02 kg total. Independently rounded entries can appear inconsistent; the correct output can be “clarify rounded mass,” rather than an accusation or automatic rejection. Legitimate 99.6% rounded to 100% remains distinct from an impossible claimed 100.4%.

### Final refinements and kill gates

Treat “comparable” as **internally comparable supplied fields**, not verified content. Preserve approved product/packaging scope, precision, evidence references and the rule behind each question.

Use proportionate, human-approved clarification. Include transcription, reviewer correction, duplicate questions and supplier response work in a future total-effort comparison. False authenticity/compliance, automatic bidder exclusion or hidden boundary inference is rejected.

There is no demonstrated informational advantage over the matched spreadsheet. Equal outputs also do not establish equal usability or effort.

**Allowed pitch:** “A traceable ledger that identifies specific clarification needs in supplied declarations.”

**Unverified:** document parsing, authenticity, equivalent-evidence interpretation, buyer bottleneck, supplier burden, changed purchasing, actual recycled-content uplift and emissions.

## G2 — OffcutPlan

**Owner and action:** a maker or workshop operator allocates inspected, usable offcuts of one known untreated profile to a fixed nonstructural order, then approves the plan.

This is the narrowed **OffcutPlan** workflow. It is not the earlier mixed-deconstruction salvage or marketplace proposal.

**Causal chain:** known eligible inventory and required order → valid allocation → approved physical execution → potentially less virgin stock actually cut. A plan alone is neither a purchase nor completed reuse.

Material-aware scrap inventory, design and cut previews already exist in research. XR-penter is direct prior art involving casual makers; it does not establish industrial workshop demand. [XR-penter primary paper](https://arxiv.org/abs/2501.15413)

| Simulated stakeholder | Damaging objection |
|---|---|
| Workshop owner/incumbent | Experienced workers may already reuse convenient stock or choose best-fit. Why pay to remeasure and enter every scrap? |
| Floorworker | Saved timber may add inspection, rack searches, setups, cuts and storage of useless leftovers. |
| Government/taxpayer | The parts must satisfy the same required order and actually substitute new stock. Unnecessary scrap projects do not demonstrate substitution. |
| Auditor/people advocate | Count physical IDs, usable segments, whole purchases, kerf and leftovers; dimensions alone do not certify condition. |

The proposed payer is the workshop production/materials budget. Workers bear inspection, sorting, measurement and retrieval costs; no buyer or workflow bottleneck was observed. A later trial needs accepted parts meeting the same order, virgin stock actually cut, operator time, rejected pieces and useful residual inventory. Stock entries must be updated or reserved after each job. Extra scrap-based production and growing unusable inventories can undermine substitution; neither effect was measured.

### Exact surviving evidence

The reference tracks whole new stock required from a declared catalog, cuts, retrievals and conservation. If a 500 mm part comes from a 1,000 mm new board with a 3 mm separation cut, the requirement is the **whole 1,000 mm board**, with 497 mm retained. This is not a claim that money was spent.

| Named diagnostic | Candidate | Original first-fit | Exploratory offcut-first best-fit |
|---|---|---|---|
| Separation kerf | 1,000 mm new stock; two cuts/two retrievals | Same | Same |
| Greedy trap | 0 mm; two cuts/two retrievals | 1,000 mm; three cuts/three retrievals | Matches candidate |
| Perfect fit | 0 mm; no cuts/two retrievals | Same | Same |
| Ten tiny pieces | 0 mm; no cuts/ten retrievals | Same | Same |
| Ten lengths/five part types | Falls back to first-fit: 0 mm, nine cuts/eight retrievals | Matches candidate | 0 mm, ten cuts/nine retrievals |

In the trap, 903 mm and 603 mm stock can meet 600/500/400 mm parts with 3 mm kerf: the 603 mm piece supplies 600 mm, and the 903 mm piece supplies 500 + 400 mm. First-fit can consume the wrong stock first; its failure is not proof the catalog is infeasible.

The reference matched 32 tiny purchase-objective oracles. The larger ten-length/five-type case exceeded 20,000 search states and disclosed a first-fit fallback. It supplied no optimum proof.

The best-fit policy was an **exploratory post-hoc** response to the featured trap. Every named candidate outcome was matched by at least one reasonable simple policy, but this does not establish universal equivalence, nor may the best policy be selected silently after seeing a new outcome. A fixed baseline portfolio must be declared before a fresh comparison.

### Handling tradeoff

Ten tiny offcuts need ten retrievals and no cuts. The virgin-only diagnostic uses two new boards, two retrievals and ten cuts. That is eight additional retrievals and ten fewer cuts:

| Assumed time per retrieval/cut | Candidate minus virgin-only time |
|---|---:|
| 15 seconds / 5 seconds | +70 seconds |
| 5 seconds / 15 seconds | −110 seconds |

These assumptions can reverse the result. Neither is measured labour or a complete cost model. The competent offcut baseline may already match the candidate, and virgin-only is a weak comparator for added optimisation value.

### Final refinements and kill gates

Keep fixed order, known material, inspected segments, physical reservations, quantities and kerf explicit. Show whole new-stock requirements, cuts, retrievals and useful leftovers together. Freeze first-fit/best-fit policies before unseen cases and expose search limits.

Duplicate allocation, unsuitable stock release, changed orders or invalid fits require repair. Heuristic failure cannot prove infeasibility; fallback cannot prove global optimality. Net cost, actual reuse, future inventory value and industrial scalability remain unverified.

The frame/truss circularity case concerns chipping and transport; its benefits cannot validate dimensional allocation.

**Allowed pitch:** “An auditable fixed-order cut plan with explicit material and handling tradeoffs.”

## A2 — ClimateOdds

**Owner and action:** a teacher or climate-education facilitator runs a short lesson and inspects reasoning on new probability examples.

**Causal chain:** faithful rehearsal → improved interpretation on unseen examples → potentially better later preparedness reasoning. Only content logic was tested.

BOM guidance distinguishes forecast likelihood from rainfall amount and guarantees. It supports the lesson's subject matter, not a demonstrated audience misconception or educational effect. [BOM long-range forecast guidance](https://www.bom.gov.au/resources/learn-and-explore/climate-knowledge-centre/using-long-range-forecasts)

| Simulated stakeholder | Damaging objection |
|---|---|
| Teacher/program buyer | Why introduce another tool instead of equal-time BOM facts and guided questions? Count preparation and marking. |
| Learner/behavioural scientist | The interaction may teach an answer pattern or increase confidence without transfer. |
| Household/people advocate | Costs, budgets and preferences can justify different actions without changing probability understanding. |
| Auditor/regulator | Correct content is not an executed lesson, learning study or operational forecast recommendation. |

A school or education programme is a possible payer; a free public-resource model is also possible. Neither demand nor curriculum fit was established. Teacher preparation/feedback, learner reading and numeracy, interface familiarity and order effects can alter the apparent result. Future outcomes must separate unseen reasoning and delayed retention from enjoyment, confidence and task preference. More confident misunderstanding is a risk to test, not an observed result.

### Exact surviving evidence

The reference checked six probability values: 0, 0.2, 0.5, 0.7, 0.8 and 1. It does not infer percentage rainfall increase or daily rain chance from an above-threshold seasonal probability.

Formal model probability one is distinguished from a reported forecast guaranteeing weather.

For an explicitly defined fictional loss event with probability 0.8 and unprotected loss 100, protection costing 10 minimises expected cost, while protection costing 90 does not. At probability 0.5, protection costing 50 ties the expected unprotected loss of 50: two actions can be valid.

These are declared payoff examples. Above-median rainfall probability alone does not define a household's losses or one universally correct response.

There were zero teachers or learners, no target-class misconception survey, no delivered lesson and no independent transfer or retention responses. Checking proposed control labels did not establish implemented equivalent content or matched expectations.

### Final refinements and kill gates

Use equal relevant facts/exposure and a competent guided worksheet baseline. Score unseen probability explanations separately from action preferences. Use parallel forms, neutral recruitment and blind scoring; measure expected benefit before exposure and confidence afterward.

Semantic errors require repair. Repeating trained questions, enjoyment, confidence or simulated respondents cannot establish learning. An inaccessible interface would need correction, but no finished interface was audited here.

**Allowed pitch:** “A content-faithful, testable climate-probability lesson with an unexecuted learning-validation plan.”

**Unverified:** classroom demand, participant access, transfer, retention, teaching burden, expectancy, behaviour and adaptation outcomes.

## W2 — ApartmentFOGO

**Owner and action:** a building manager or waste coordinator shortlists a candidate bin location or identifies the permission, collection or access question that must be resolved.

**Causal chain:** entered location constraints → correct conditional shortlist/blocker → authorised physical pilot → measured separation with acceptable contamination. Distance alone does not establish that chain.

Port Phillip describes space-dependent services and apartment access barriers. This supports the local problem, not a causal estimate linking shorter paths to participation. The official page was read by a worker earlier; a later root fetch returned 403, so access coverage is limited. [Port Phillip FOGO service](https://www.portphillip.vic.gov.au/council-services/waste-recycling-and-rubbish/dispose-of-your-food-and-garden-organics-fogo/)

| Simulated stakeholder | Damaging objection |
|---|---|
| Manager/incumbent inspector | Three placements may be resolved in one visit. Who grants permission, and why encode the decision? |
| Resident/disability advocate | A shorter average route can conceal steps, locked doors or an unusable route for one group. |
| Cleaner/collector | Bin opening, washing, movement, storage and collection can create burdens not represented by resident distance. |
| Auditor/finance | Area and access labels are not geometry; equal weekly service totals can still conceal overflow. |

An owners corporation or building waste budget is a possible payer, while residents, caretakers and collectors may receive different costs and benefits. Cleaning, odour management, bin transport and changing access arrangements need owners. More captured organics can reflect occupancy or waste growth rather than diversion. A later comparable pilot should record residual-bin food fraction, organics per occupied unit, contamination, collection frequency and resident/cleaner burden; none was measured here.

### Exact surviving evidence

The reference checks scalar weekly capacity, footprint area and entered permission/access flags. It has no real spatial or route solver.

Candidate and competent inspection agreed on all eight core cases and selected the same 10 m feasible location. Nearest-only selected a 3 m locked location; that is a weak ablation, not competent practice.

Unknown or omitted access remains unresolved, and invalid numbers do not pass. Independent people challenges also confirmed that additional steps, door-width or inspection metadata do not alter the supplied-label result. A sufficient area does not establish that a bin's width/depth, lid or swept movement fits.

Government's temporal counterexample survives: 800 L per week with two 480 L collections passes aggregate weekly service capacity, but a 600 L burst before one collection exceeds the 480 L storage. This is an omitted condition, not an arithmetic error in the stated weekly model.

### Final refinements and kill gates

Call the output “passes supplied checks; verification required.” Record evidence owner, date and source. A later spatial prototype needs per-group routes, doors, usable shape, lid/swept clearances and collector movement. Storage claims need peak accumulation between actual collection windows.

Keep bin capacity and service frequency equal when isolating placement. Count resident, cleaner and collection burdens. Unknown permission, subgroup exclusion or unresolved overflow prevents a physical-feasibility claim.

Do not infer approval, accessibility, participation, contamination reduction, diversion or methane savings from flags, area or distance.

**Allowed pitch:** “A testable placement/checklist mockup that exposes supplied blockers and missing verification.”

## Placebo, trust and future validation

The people review drew a necessary distinction:

| Comparison | What it can examine | What it cannot establish here |
|---|---|---|
| Component ablation | Dependence on particular rules, constraints or allocation logic | Psychological placebo without people. |
| Competent active baseline | Fixture outcomes under identical facts and objectives | Human effort, adoption or superiority beyond the tested cases. |
| Same correct output, varied presentation | A future test of confidence/authority effects | Effects before actual participants and expectancy measures exist. |
| Same-content lesson/control | A future test of instructional components | Matched expectations merely because duration or appearance match. |

A less informative generic output changes the information available; it does not isolate expectancy. Greater confidence with unchanged correctness would not establish the claimed mechanism, but no such human result was observed. Small-study nonsignificance would also not prove no effect.

The relevant primary work warns about expectation matching and expectancy-related cognitive-training results. It does not show that these five projects are placebos. [Boot et al.](https://journals.sagepub.com/doi/10.1177/1745691613491271), [Foroughi et al.](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/27325761/)

Digital accessibility also needs an implemented product: keyboard operation, readable units, low-bandwidth alternatives and usable exports were proposed requirements. A future keyboard audit could expose barriers but would not prove accessibility for every disability. [W3C keyboard guidance](https://www.w3.org/WAI/WCAG22/Understanding/keyboard.html)

| Beating the original simple baseline establishes practical value. | Revise. | Stronger simple policies matched showcase wins; they were post-hoc and need fresh predeclared comparisons. |
| Baseline parity proves the whole project worthless or placebo. | Reject. | It limits demonstrated informational advantage; usability, workload and expectancy remain unknown. |
| No stakeholders means the concepts cannot qualify for the event. | Reject. | Realistic testable mockups are permitted; eligibility and causal evidence are separate. |
| Unmeasured handling must outweigh saved material. | Reject. | Hypothetical parameters can reverse the tradeoff; neither net effect was measured. |
| Unknowns or heuristic/model failure can receive a reassuring pass or definitive real-world-impossibility label. | Reject. | Inputs, objectives, resolution and incomplete search must be explicit. |
| Passing the diagnostics validates five finished applications. | Reject. | The completed artefacts were reference logic, challenges and claim revisions. |

The final order of work is:

1. **All five:** freeze fair active baselines and unseen diagnostic suites. Report ties, failures and scope limits as well as showcase wins.
2. **E1/G2:** advance the smallest auditable allocation demonstration. Keep resolution, mandatory requirements, search status and material/handling tradeoffs visible.
3. **G1/W2:** narrow to precise questions and blockers; their tested logic already matches competent spreadsheet/inspection practice.
4. **A2:** preserve semantic correctness and the future transfer protocol. Remove learning gains until people are tested.
5. **All five:** label supplied inputs and unmeasured downstream links. Do not turn event targets or fixture deltas into projected climate impact.

This is a completed assessment of the five **mechanisms**, not completed human or product validation. Further product refinements are proposed work.
