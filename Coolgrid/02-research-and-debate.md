# Cross-priority research and earlier shortlist debate

**Historical analysis date: 2 October 2026, Australia/Sydney.** This document records the research and first two-reviewer shortlist debate before the later stakeholder and executable-mechanism assessment. It preserves that stage's arguments, corrections and conditional decisions.

Read [01 — Original CoolGrid analysis](01-coolgrid-analysis.md) for the assessment that prompted this wider search. Read [03 — Stakeholder and mechanism validation](03-stakeholder-validation.md) for the later tests, stronger baselines and final claim limits.

> **Supersession notice:** The ranking below is an earlier build-priority shortlist, not demonstrated superiority. The later analysis in document 03 supersedes any implication that FleetCharge or OffcutPlan has established an optimisation advantage over competent existing practice. Do not quote this earlier ranking as evidence of stakeholder demand, user benefit, climate impact or winning likelihood.

## Event standard and research scope

The event asked teams to turn at least one climate priority into a practical solution people or institutions could test. A finished commercial product was not expected. A working proof of concept or a realistic, testable clickable mockup was permitted. Real inputs and representative-user review could strengthen relevance; they were not a uniform eligibility prerequisite for a labelled mockup. The [participant guide](https://hackjunction.app/participate/52/guide) and [organizer's mockup clarification](https://discord.com/channels/1534362591739252866/1541023353492606977/threads/1555253800648974497) were the event basis recorded in the context dossier.

| Priority | Event framing | Event-provided 2035 target | Candidates researched |
|---|---|---|---|
| Electrification | Replace fossil transport, heating or machinery with electric power | 35% electrification | E1 FleetCharge; E2 StrataChargePack |
| Zero Waste & Methane Reduction | Reduce waste and methane from decomposition | Slow waste growth by 50% | W1 WasteReason; W2 ApartmentFOGO |
| Resilient Cities & Buildings | Reduce building energy use or improve resilience to extremes | Cut building-sector energy use by 25% | B1 CoolGrid backup checker; B2 FloodPack |
| Green Industrialization | Reuse and recycle materials in production | 15% circular materials use | G1 RecycledEvidence; G2 OffcutPlan |
| Awareness Across All Areas | Farmer/land-manager adaptation and accessible climate education | Climate-resilient farming and climate education | A1 WaterReserve; A2 ClimateOdds |

These targets came from the event materials. Their baselines and denominators were not fully specified, and this research did not independently verify them as climate statistics. Addressing one part of a priority was allowed; the final five did not have to cover all five priorities artificially. The definitions and scope clarification were recorded from the [challenge post](https://discord.com/channels/1534362591739252866/1541022314626293830/1542851315405951089) and [mentor answer](https://discord.com/channels/1534362591739252866/1541023353492606977/threads/1555206302945509396).

The judging weights were **30% alignment, 30% build quality, 20% creativity and 20% presentation clarity**. These informed qualitative comparisons, not invented scores or win probabilities. Construction started on **2 October at 09:00 AEST**, and the planning cutoff was **4 October at 21:00 AEDT**. The guide's explicit Sunday AEDT deadline was used because the landing page's blanket UTC+10 label conflicted with it. External resources and material AI use required disclosure. These constraints are documented in the [participant guide](https://hackjunction.app/participate/52/guide).

The research deliberately looked for one action owner, one bounded mechanism and an observable test using public information or clearly illustrative inputs. It did not select or provision commercial services, contact people, install infrastructure or measure actual outcomes.

### What was and was not established at this stage

- Official guidance and primary research could establish a real class of constraint and an existing workflow.
- Public data availability did not establish that it matched the proposed user's decision inputs.
- A believable synthetic demonstration could test arithmetic, rules or a proposed workflow.
- A successful fixture could not establish demand, usability, adoption, ROI, learning, waste reduction, health benefit or emissions savings.
- No prototype was built in this earlier debate. No representative user, private operational record or actual climate outcome was available.
- Source evidence was not treated as innovation validation. A source often strengthened the incumbent baseline more than the new idea.

Every position was conditional. Kitchen access could promote W1; inspected workshop inventory could strengthen G2; learners or a teacher could strengthen A2; representative building inputs could strengthen W2; and verified facility specifications could promote B1. Those were possible ranking changes, not available evidence.

## Candidate dossiers

The following records combine the candidate research with the earlier adjudicated refinements. “Evidence” describes what the cited sources supported. “Hypothesis” describes the proposed additional value. The tests are proposed tests at this stage, not completed experiments.

### E1 — FleetCharge

**Priority and action owner.** Electrification. Council small-fleet manager: assign overnight charging sessions for the next operating day.

**Evidence and conjecture.** US DOE explicitly identifies site-power ceilings, departure requirements and changing duty schedules as managed-fleet-charging constraints. Direct depot-scheduling research addresses off-duty charging under grid capacity limits. The unvalidated inference was: A council fleet may benefit from an explainable next-day schedule, but no real council duty log or binding constraint has been observed. The main limiting assumption was that changing a schedule is the binding electrification barrier and yields a useful improvement over competent manual capped charging; capital, vehicle suitability or lack of overnight slack may dominate.

**Existing baseline and differentiation hypothesis.** DOE guidance already describes immediate charging, manually entered arrival/departure schedules, static power limits and dynamic load management. Depot optimisation is established research. Compare both immediate charging and a competent cap-compliant spreadsheet/static schedule. The proposed additional value was: For a small operator, expose the precise missed departure or power constraint and the minimum operational change needed, with an auditable schedule. Added usability over manual cap-compliant planning remains to be tested.

**Minimum demonstration and input availability.** Eight synthetic vehicles, 15-minute intervals, arrivals/departures, starting/required energy, charge limits and one site cap; compare immediate charging and a manual/static cap-compliant schedule on identical duty rows. Include a genuinely infeasible case. Public NLR dataset contains 81 aggregate 24-hour, 15-minute heavy-duty depot profiles for three fleets, sizes, charging strategies and summary profile types. It supplies realistic aggregate context, not per-vehicle council duties or validation of rescheduling. Use labelled synthetic vehicle rows; actual duty logs and electrical limits are unavailable. Weekend scope: feasible as local schedule simulator or clickable mockup before 4 October 2026 21:00 AEDT; hardware control and optimality at scale excluded.

**Impact hypothesis.** Feasible charge schedule -> fewer charging-related missed duties or peak-cap violations -> lower operational barrier to replacing fossil vehicles. Actual replacement and emissions benefit require a later pilot. This remained an untested impact chain.

**Earlier final refinement.** One depot; remove live control, tariffs and emissions claims. Include building-load-adjusted site power, charger exclusivity/compatibility, vehicle rates, connection windows and required departure energy.

**Baseline and validation conditions.**

- **Competent comparator:** Immediate charging plus cap-compliant manual/deadline-first scheduling on identical inputs and objectives.
- **Prototype test:** Independent validator checks each emitted interval and total energy, charger use, arrival/departure windows and power. Hand-check small cases; compare explanation correctness or reviewer effort where schedules are equivalent.
- **Go condition:** Every emitted schedule respects the stated constraints; one useful rescheduling or explanation/task-effort benefit is demonstrated.
- **Failure condition:** Any constraint violation, silent change to requirements, unsupported optimality claim, or sole comparison to immediate charging.
- **Later validation:** Replay anonymised real duty logs and compare operational feasibility, staff effort and measured site peaks.
- **Explicitly unproved:** EV adoption, savings and avoided emissions.

**Earlier decision:** Retain conditionally. Synthetic data suffices for a prototype. Real depot need, duty patterns and building demand remain unverified.

**Sources and access limits recorded on 2 October 2026:**

- [US DOE managed EV charging for federal fleets](https://www.energy.gov/cmei/femp/managed-ev-charging-federal-fleets) — Direct official baseline describes power ceilings, arrival/departure scheduling, static manual inputs needing updates as duties change, and dynamic management. **Access:** Primary page read.
- [Electric vehicle charge scheduling with flexible service operations](https://arxiv.org/abs/2201.03972) — Original depot research jointly schedules off-duty charging and flexible departures under operational and infrastructure constraints; not evidence of council adoption. **Access:** Opened in the research record.
- [NLR heavy-duty electric fleet depot charging load profiles](https://data.nlr.gov/submissions/162) — Public 2021 research dataset offers 81 15-minute aggregate depot profiles; three fleets, fleet sizes, strategies and average/minimum/peak-day types. **Access:** Primary indexed dataset description reviewed. Catalog updated 12 March 2026; aggregate loads do not reveal individual vehicle departure constraints.
- [Electric Vehicle Fleet and Charging Infrastructure Planning](https://arxiv.org/abs/2306.10178) — Background on spatial dispatch, service levels and vehicle/charger scaling; not direct support for fixed-depot overnight power-cap scheduling. **Access:** Primary abstract read. Downgraded from direct evidence to background.

### E2 — StrataChargePack

**Priority and action owner.** Electrification. Owners-corporation committee secretary: assemble one staged EV-readiness proposal with responsible owners and missing evidence.

**Evidence and conjecture.** NSW official process requires planning/feasibility, approvals, installation and operation, including evidence and different stakeholders. The unvalidated inference was: Unclear handoffs may slow a committee even when members want charging; no observed committee evidence. The main limiting assumption was that coordination rather than money, technical constraints or owner opposition is the bottleneck; strong public baseline may fully solve it.

**Existing baseline and differentiation hypothesis.** NSW five-stage EV-ready guide, resident survey, meeting motions, feasibility checklist, example reports and building costing tool; Australian Government strata toolkits also cover surveys/handoffs. The proposed additional value was: Very weak unless a stage-owned, auditable missing-decision queue reduces handoff failures beyond these templates. Do not sell another readiness checklist.

**Minimum demonstration and input availability.** One fictional apartment scheme, resident interest summary and six missing/complete evidence tasks; create committee-ready request pack with accountable roles. Public guides/templates; electrical capacity, parking title/rights, budget and actual survey responses private/unknown. Use synthetic inputs. Current NSW grant is closed. Weekend scope: easy realistic mockup; differentiation risk high.

**Impact hypothesis.** Clearer evidence/handoffs -> better prepared committee proposal -> possibly faster consideration of shared EV infrastructure -> lower charging access barrier. This remained an untested impact chain.

**Earlier final refinement.** Reopen only as a narrow evidence-ownership queue for a demonstrated handoff gap; no grant, legal or electrical approval automation.

**Baseline and validation conditions.**

- **Competent comparator:** NSW five-stage materials and Australian Government strata toolkit.
- **Prototype test:** Same proposal with official templates versus queue; compare missed dependencies, ownership and effort.
- **Go condition:** A material distinct handoff/effort benefit.
- **Failure condition:** Generic checklist repackage without a demonstrated gap.
- **Later validation:** Committee task-completion and meeting outcome pilot without treating approval as guaranteed.
- **Explicitly unproved:** Legal permission, electrical feasibility, cost, grant eligibility, EV adoption.

**Earlier decision:** Discard current generic pitch. Discard is shortlist prioritisation, not proof that all strata workflow adaptations are worthless. Grant closure is separate and cannot be presented as a live benefit.

### W1 — WasteReason

**Priority and action owner.** Zero Waste & Methane Reduction. Campus canteen kitchen supervisor: choose one reversible production buffer or staged batch for the next service of a repeated dish.

**Evidence and conjecture.** EPA-supported Catering Toolkit distinguishes spoilage, preparation, serving and plate waste; it links excess production to accurate counts/forecasts and recommends per-meal audits. It also notes staff-time and organisational barriers. The unvalidated inference was: Cooked, unsold serving surplus may be controllable through a next-service buffer. Its dominance over spoilage, trimming, portion mismatch or demand uncertainty is not observed; validate the cause before choosing the action. The main limiting assumption was that overproduction is the dominant controllable loss and staff can stage batches; tighter buffers may cause stockouts. The existing toolkit may already be sufficient.

**Existing baseline and differentiation hypothesis.** BinTrim already provides waste assessments, action plans, tracking and circularity analysis. EPA-supported Catering Toolkit already supplies reason categories, waste-per-meal measurement and category-specific actions including production forecasting. Generic logs, recommendations and dashboards overlap this baseline. The proposed additional value was: A specific repeated-dish decision rehearsal: compare unchanged production with one adjustable buffer/staged batch, show uncertainty, and require both cooked unsold surplus and unmet demand outcomes. Extra value over a competent toolkit-based spreadsheet remains a hypothesis.

**Minimum demonstration and input availability.** Seven synthetic services for one dish with attendance, portions requested/prepared/served, cooked unsold surplus and stockouts; one user-set buffer/staging change. Separate plate waste and preparation trimmings from excess production. Replay a held-out fixture only as a functional demonstration. Public toolkit and deidentified aggregate BinTrim reports exist; neither supplies dish/service-level demand logs. Public Reports suppress small postcode groups. Synthetic rows demonstrate accounting; useful prediction needs longer real POS/service records and waste weights. Weekend scope: feasible as one-dish log and experiment mockup; no ML prediction or food safety advice needed.

**Impact hypothesis.** Identify avoidable cooked unsold surplus -> trial a controlled production buffer or later batch -> reduce edible surplus while tracking service shortfall -> possible lower disposal of organics. Methane benefit depends on the actual disposal pathway. This remained an untested impact chain.

**Earlier final refinement.** Replace reason-log novelty and automated forecasting with a supervised experiment; reconcile prepared/served/discarded quantities, separate avoidable surplus from other waste, and record stockouts/unmet demand.

**Baseline and validation conditions.**

- **Competent comparator:** EPA-supported Catering Toolkit, BinTrim action workflow, ordinary prep tally, and unchanged preparation/competent staging heuristic.
- **Prototype test:** Input reconciliation and blind supervisor task; later measure avoidable waste per meal AND stockouts/unmet demand across comparable services.
- **Go condition:** A supervisor identifies one justified test beyond what the toolkit/plain log already provides, with manageable logging burden and demand guardrail.
- **Failure condition:** Generic reason categories presented as novel; fixture replay claimed as causal savings; scraps converted into recoverable servings; fewer prepared portions called success without unmet-demand data.
- **Later validation:** Repeated-menu prospective trial with kg avoidable serving waste per meal, unmet demand and staff effort, controlling attendance/menu. Audit first: if portion or spoilage losses dominate, change the mechanism rather than force a production cut.
- **Explicitly unproved:** Demand forecasting, real waste or methane avoided, dominant cause, staff adoption or any toolkit-relative advantage.

**Earlier decision:** Defer / reserve. Kitchen access can move this onto the shortlist. Without it, W2 has a somewhat stronger documented local-barrier case, though neither has established behavior improvement.

**Sources and access limits recorded on 2 October 2026:**

Public Reports are aggregated and deidentified, with small postcode groups suppressed. **Access:** Primary page read.

### W2 — ApartmentFOGO

**Priority and action owner.** Zero Waste & Methane Reduction. Apartment building manager: choose one equally accessible food-organics collection layout and prepare a measured pilot.

**Evidence and conjecture.** Current Port Phillip service differentiates properties with room for extra bins from those using communal hubs; larger apartment buildings faced caddy-delivery access restrictions. The unvalidated inference was: Within-building bin co-location and access constraints may lower food-scrap separation; geometry alone cannot predict participation. The main limiting assumption was that access distance is binding rather than habits, private collection, odour or service cost; moving bins may require approval.

**Existing baseline and differentiation hypothesis.** Council FOGO collection, communal hubs, caddies/posters and historical high-rise feasibility studies; building waste-management plans. The proposed additional value was: Compare a few feasible placements under actual door/access/space constraints and turn the preferred layout into an audit-ready pilot, instead of another sorting guide.

**Minimum demonstration and input availability.** One explicitly synthetic floor/bin-room diagram, residual-waste location and three proposed organics positions; show path-distance inequity, space/access checks and pilot audit sheet. Current council service/rules public; actual floor plans/access/collection capacities not public. Illustrative layout accepted for mockup. Old SV high-rise PDF is unavailable. Weekend scope: feasible small layout mockup and audit plan; no building-code certification or provider selection.

**Impact hypothesis.** Equal practical access and measurable pilot -> more separated food organics with acceptable contamination -> possible lower landfill organics and decomposition methane. This remained an untested impact chain.

**Earlier final refinement.** One building, known candidate spaces and stated capacity/access/collection constraints. Keep capacity comparable across alternatives; label illustrative geometry. Remove participation/recovery uplift claims.

**Baseline and validation conditions.**

- **Competent comparator:** Current building waste plan, manager inspection and local collection/access guidance on the same layout.
- **Prototype test:** Hand-check paths, doors/permissions, usable footprint, bin capacity and collection movement. A later matched-occupancy audit measures residual organics and contamination.
- **Go condition:** One correct useful feasibility distinction or next verification action, with all supplied constraints respected.
- **Failure condition:** Geometry treated as permission/approval, ignored collection or mobility constraints, or hypothetical placement presented as measured behavior improvement.
- **Later validation:** Pre/post residual-bin food fraction, organics kg/occupied unit and contamination audits, recording occupancy and collection frequency.
- **Explicitly unproved:** Recovery uplift, emissions avoided, planning/fire-code compliance.

**Earlier decision:** Refine as a conditional finalist. No real plan, manager, applicable rules or occupancy audit confirmed. Synthetic layout is allowed; representative usefulness remains unknown.

### B1 — CoolGrid facility backup-load checker

**Priority and action owner.** Resilient Cities & Buildings. Community-facility manager: check which designated refuge-room circuits can run through one defined power outage.

**Evidence and conjecture.** Australian Government says solar-only systems usually shut down in outages and batteries require backup configuration; backed-up circuits/phases and inverter capacity matter. The unvalidated inference was: A facility may assume it can cool residents during a blackout without checking supported circuits, state of charge and sustained cooling load. The main limiting assumption was that electrical endurance is mistaken for a safe functioning refuge; HVAC startup/load under heat and access/occupancy are unknown.

**Existing baseline and differentiation hypothesis.** Government solar/battery backup guidance and engineering load calculations; Cooling and greening Melbourne already maps heat vulnerability. The proposed additional value was: Explain one facility's backup circuit/load failure and compare load/reserve changes; do not replicate regional heat mapping or score electrification-related mortality.

**Minimum demonstration and input availability.** Defined six-hour outage, one cooled room, three labelled illustrative load profiles and battery usable-energy/power/reserve settings; show energy exhaustion and power-limit/circuit failures. Public backup guidance and map baseline; verified facility wiring, appliance loads, installed backup ability and indoor thermal behaviour unavailable. Inputs must be illustrative or measured with clear provenance. Weekend scope: feasible as labelled load checker/mockup; no electrical-control integration or thermal model claim.

**Impact hypothesis.** Identify unsupported loads/insufficient reserve -> facility plans engineering assessment or revised refuge operation -> possible longer cooling continuity. This does not establish safe temperature, hub occupancy or mortality reduction. This remained an untested impact chain.

**Earlier final refinement.** Explicit usable charge, reserve, losses, startup/running load, connected circuits and inverter power; unknown or unsupported configuration yields assessment-required. No heat-safety, refuge certification or mortality claim.

**Baseline and validation conditions.**

- **Competent comparator:** Installer load worksheet, manual backup spreadsheet and facility documentation.
- **Prototype test:** Hand-computed energy/power cases and missing-input cases; verify no unsupported configuration gets a pass.
- **Go condition:** Correctly expose a concrete configuration/energy-versus-power error and next verification action.
- **Failure condition:** Electrical runtime implies safe indoor conditions, unknown configuration passes, or tool merely redraws runtime multiplication.
- **Later validation:** Qualified installer circuit verification and logged outage/controlled-load tests; indoor temperatures and refuge operations require separate validation.
- **Explicitly unproved:** Safe indoor temperature, residents served, deaths avoided or any generic 25% resilience improvement.

**Earlier decision:** Defer / reserve. Representative specifications or manager/expert review can promote it. They are not an event eligibility gate for a labelled mockup.

**Sources and access limits recorded on 2 October 2026:**

- [Australian Government battery guidance](https://www.energy.gov.au/solar/get-know-solar-technology/batteries) — Backup requires suitable configuration; islandable solar/battery can recharge during outage; backup may cover selected circuits/phases. **Access:** Opened in the research record.
- [Cooling and greening Melbourne map](https://www.planning.vic.gov.au/guides-and-resources/Data-spatial-and-insights/cooling-and-greening-melbourne-map) — Public map already combines metropolitan heat/vegetation/vulnerability data. **Access:** Search-indexed source; full-page review not claimed here.

### G1 — RecycledEvidence

**Priority and action owner.** Green Industrialization. Public procurement officer: identify missing recycled-content evidence in submitted product bids and request specific clarifications.

**Evidence and conjecture.** The current 2024 DCCEEW guide specifies mass-based recycled-content evidence, separation of product from packaging and attention to unsupported claims; Appendix E describes reporting fields. The unvalidated inference was: A procurement reviewer may struggle with inconsistent declarations, but no buyer has identified this as the binding obstacle. The main limiting assumption was that evidence review is the bottleneck rather than price, supply availability or performance specifications.

**Existing baseline and differentiation hypothesis.** Current official procurement guide and reporting example, tender templates and a competent spreadsheet implementing the same checks. The proposed additional value was: Make missing denominators, evidence and inconsistent product/packaging claims easier to detect and explain than a competent spreadsheet; generate precise clarification questions. No supplier ranking or compliance certification.

**Minimum demonstration and input availability.** Six fictional product declarations with virgin/recycled/unknown mass, separate product/packaging entries and evidence dates; flag inconsistencies and export clarification questions. Official guide available; realistic bids proprietary. Use deliberately synthetic declarations. Do not select or engage suppliers. Weekend scope: feasible local form/evidence ledger; no paid-standard text, automatic legal certification or supplier matching.

**Impact hypothesis.** More auditable claims -> better specified procurement and fewer unsupported circularity assertions -> potential increased verified recycled-material purchasing. This remained an untested impact chain.

**Earlier final refinement.** Use structured manual/CSV input, human-approved product boundary, recycled and total masses, goods-versus-packaging separation and evidence references. Remove autonomous document interpretation, authenticity, compliance and circularity-ranking claims.

**Baseline and validation conditions.**

### Access and verification caveats

The audit distinguished direct reading, indexed text and failed access. The root read the procurement PDF text; its screenshot request failed. ## Final validation conditions from the earlier shortlist

The earlier shortlist supported development only under these conditions:

1. **E1:** Validate each interval against connection windows, charge rates, charger exclusivity/compatibility, building-load-adjusted power and departure energy. Do not silently relax duties or call a heuristic failure mathematical infeasibility. If a competent schedule matches, added explanation or task-effort value still needs an independent test.
2. **G1:** Compare the same structured declarations and guide-backed rules with a competent spreadsheet. Separate missing evidence, unknown scope and contradictory masses. Never authenticate evidence or rank incomparable declarations automatically.
3. **G2:** Keep one known profile, inspected usable lengths, quantities and kerf, with human release. Check conservation and duplicate allocation. Compare the same required order, new stock, cuts and handling; no actual reuse or optimisation claim from an unexecuted plan.
4. **A2:** Keep one probability-learning goal and faithful content. Compare equal exposure to the same BOM information, use unseen different-format questions and separate usability from learning. Without participants, supply a testable lesson and test plan rather than a learning-benefit claim.
5. **W2:** Keep capacity and service conditions comparable. Check paths, access/permissions, usable footprint and collection movement. An illustrative layout can test supplied constraints; it cannot certify approval or predict participation, contamination or methane reduction.

The reserve candidates had equally concrete promotion conditions: W1 needed a useful supervisor-led experiment beyond toolkit practice; B1 needed a meaningful configuration/energy-versus-power verification task; A1 needed a distinct observation/remeasurement benefit. E2 and B2 could reopen only around a demonstrated missing handoff or accessibility/offline failure that the incumbent did not already solve.

No source citation could replace those comparisons. Nor could consensus replace a user test. At this earlier stage, **unmet demand, novelty across all alternatives, baseline superiority, completed core functionality, user benefit, climate/health/economic/learning outcomes and winning likelihood remained unestablished**.

For the later executable comparisons, independent counterexamples, repairs and stronger baseline results, use [03 — Stakeholder and mechanism validation](03-stakeholder-validation.md). Its claim limits govern the current interpretation of these earlier candidates.
