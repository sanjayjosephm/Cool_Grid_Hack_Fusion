## Purpose and challenge

Climate Hack-tion is a fully online student hackathon, funded by the European Union, ahead of COP31. Its theme is **Build for 2035**: turn at least one climate priority into a practical solution that people, communities, or institutions could test and use. Working language: **English**. Junction handles registration, teams, submissions, and judging; Discord handles community support and mentoring; Webex is planned for the closing ceremony. [Participant guide](https://hackjunction.app/participate/52/guide)

A finished commercial product is not expected. Show the intended user, how the solution works, how its potential impact could be tested, and a credible route from prototype to real-world use. Teams choose their own ideas within the published priorities; no additional case or scenario was promised. [Getting-started challenge post](https://discord.com/channels/1534362591739252866/1541022314626293830/1542851315405951089), [organizer's ideation answer](https://discord.com/channels/1534362591739252866/1541023353492606977/threads/1550016683815473262)

| Priority | Event framing | Target stated in the event materials |
|---|---|---|
| Electrification | Move fossil-fuel transport, heating, or machinery to electric power | 35% electrification by 2035 |
| Zero Waste & Methane Reduction | Reduce waste and methane from its decomposition | Slow waste growth by 50% by 2035 |
| Resilient Cities & Buildings | Reduce building energy use or improve resilience to heat, floods, and other extremes | Cut building-sector energy use by 25% by 2035 |
| Green Industrialization | Reuse and recycle materials in manufacturing and production | 15% circular materials use by 2035 |
| Awareness Across All Areas | Climate adaptation for farmers/land managers or accessible climate knowledge and education | Climate-resilient farming and climate education across society by 2035 |

These are **event-provided challenge targets**, not independently verified climate statistics; baselines and denominators are not fully specified in the channel. A mentor explicitly confirmed that if a priority has multiple parts, a project may focus on **one part**. Projects may address multiple priorities, but at least one needs a clear connection. [Priority definitions](https://discord.com/channels/1534362591739252866/1541022314626293830/1542851315405951089), [scope clarification](https://discord.com/channels/1534362591739252866/1541023353492606977/threads/1555206302945509396)

## Eligibility, team size, and registration

- Each member must be **18+** and an eligible current university/TAFE student in Australia, New Zealand, the Pacific Islands, or an EU member state. Completing a degree before the graduation ceremony remains eligible under the published rules.
- Teams must have **3–5 active members**. Solo entry and teams above five are prohibited. Each participant belongs to one team.
- Teams register as Australia, New Zealand, or Pacific Islands. EU students join an eligible AU/NZ/Pacific team; **EU-only teams are prohibited**. Mixed-country projects use the country most members are from. Country ties are not explained.
- Listed Pacific jurisdictions: Fiji, Cook Islands, Federated States of Micronesia, Kiribati, Nauru, Niue, Palau, Marshall Islands, Samoa, Tonga, Tuvalu, Solomon Islands, Vanuatu, and Papua New Guinea.
- AU/NZ/Pacific citizens studying outside the region need an organizer eligibility check. Having one Australian university student does not make otherwise ineligible overseas teammates eligible.

[Competition rules](https://discord.com/channels/1534362591739252866/1541022250210431006/1542849909042974750), [guide §§1, 6](https://hackjunction.app/participate/52/guide), [overseas-member clarification](https://discord.com/channels/1534362591739252866/1541023353492606977/threads/1553621453612523541)

## Building rules, research, external resources, and AI

**Project construction begins only after Friday 2 October, 09:00 Sydney time.** Prior ideas, research, ideation, and team formation are allowed; start the submitted build from scratch. Prebuilt code, designs, graphics, images, audio, and 3D work are prohibited as submission assets. Stop building at the deadline; small bug fixes afterward are permitted, but substantial changes or new features are not. The rules do not precisely define a small fix. [Competition rules](https://discord.com/channels/1534362591739252866/1541022250210431006/1542849909042974750), [guide §8](https://hackjunction.app/participate/52/guide)

Mentor clarifications distinguish preparation from the submitted build:

- Public-dataset downloads and exploratory analysis/model suitability checks before the start were accepted as research, in response to a question conditioned on writing submission code during the event and disclosing datasets.
- Prior presentation slides may be brought as research/exploration material for developing a tangible solution during the event. This is not permission to reuse those slides as submitted assets.
- Planning the idea, software, APIs, and implementation map before the event is allowed if no part of the build starts early.

[Research answer](https://discord.com/channels/1534362591739252866/1541023353492606977/threads/1554458328367767622), [prior slides and planning answers](https://discord.com/channels/1534362591739252866/1541023353492606977/threads/1552829875646369859)

Legal outside tools, assets, templates, open-source libraries, datasets, and APIs are allowed, but **all must be disclosed**, including paid resources. Teams must comply with licenses, data/privacy rules, partner terms, and security restrictions. The precise boundary between allowed external assets and prohibited prebuilt submission work is not exhaustively defined; do not assume an unstated exemption. [Guide §8](https://hackjunction.app/participate/52/guide)

**AI coding, completion, code generation, image generation, and similar tools are permitted.** List everything used and disclose material AI use. No numerical limit on AI contribution was stated. The team remains responsible for originality, accuracy, licensing, security, and rights to generated material. The mentor specifically cautioned against generating material using another party's copyright/trademarks without permission. Disclosure may be included in a slide, repository file, or submission description. [AI answer](https://discord.com/channels/1534362591739252866/1541023353492606977/threads/1554089917359980624), [FAQ disclosure instructions](https://discord.com/channels/1534362591739252866/1541022290626609252/1542853433470750760)

## Deliverables and submission

Two accepted formats were confirmed by an organizer:

1. **Working prototype / proof of concept:** core functionality demonstrably operates; visual polish is not required.
2. **Realistic mockup / clickable prototype:** permitted for teams without a developer, for example using Figma.

[Organizer answer, 2 October](https://discord.com/channels/1534362591739252866/1541023353492606977/threads/1555253800648974497)

Submit through **Junction Project Submission** before Sunday 21:00 AEDT. The guide lists:

- Project name, one-sentence summary, selected challenge, and team nationality.
- Team members and their roles.
- Problem, target users, solution, approach, and intended impact.
- Written project description/pitch.
- **Demo/pitch video no longer than two minutes**, produced during the event; the guide also mentions slides as a format.
- Public code repository link, maintained through judging and winner announcements.
- Supporting materials, tools/datasets/APIs/AI disclosures, and declaration that the project was built at the event.

All links must open without requesting access, and the submission must be saved and visibly complete. Submit progressively to avoid last-minute upload failures. For genuine technical failure, contact `shelly.amir@gdsi.ie` **before** the deadline with timestamped evidence; special consideration is discretionary. Judging questions: `sharon.offenberger@gdsi.ie`. [Guide §§9, 16](https://hackjunction.app/participate/52/guide)

**Repository ambiguity:** the older Discord FAQ says a repository link “if any,” while the participant guide explicitly requires a public code repository. Follow the guide for planning; mockup acceptance does not establish an automatic repository exemption. **Video ambiguity:** the question about appearing on camera versus a demo-only/presentation video had no organizer answer in the reviewed thread. Do not invent a face-camera requirement. [Older FAQ](https://discord.com/channels/1534362591739252866/1541022290626609252/1542853433470750760), [unanswered video question](https://discord.com/channels/1534362591739252866/1541023353492606977/threads/1555359123258740776)

## Judging and awards

| Criterion | Weight | Evidence to make clear |
|---|---:|---|
| COP31 alignment | 30% | Explicit connection to a priority, real conditions, and why the problem matters |
| Build quality | 30% | Demonstrated and validated core functionality, or a testable prototype |
| Creativity | 20% | Original approach and inventive use of data, design, or technology |
| Presentation clarity | 20% | Plain-language explanation of problem, solution, and target audience |

Judges score independently and scores are averaged. Ties favor the higher COP31-alignment score; panel decisions are final. National awards use jury review of the highest-rated projects in each region. [Judging post](https://discord.com/channels/1534362591739252866/1541023353492606977/threads/1555365862406488064), [guide §10](https://hackjunction.app/participate/52/guide)

The event advertises a roughly A$10,000 prize pool. Listed main awards are **A$6,000 winner**, **A$3,500 runner-up**, and a **People's Choice product valued at A$1,500** (A$11,000 combined listed value, including the product). National awards are named for Australia, New Zealand, and the Pacific, but their values were not specified in the guide. People's Choice uses participant gavel voting on Junction. Prizes are per team; a nominated representative receives funds and distributes them evenly. Applicable EU financial conditions may require an equivalent in-kind award. Winners are announced on 12 October; the ceremony time conflict is noted above. [Guide §11](https://hackjunction.app/participate/52/guide), [EU award announcement](https://www.eeas.europa.eu/delegations/fiji/climate-hacktion-unites-students-build-climate-solutions-ahead-cop31_en)

Practical checklist derived from the reviewed rules:

1. Confirm all 3–5 members are eligible, individually registered, and in the actual Junction team.
2. Choose a specific priority/subproblem and user; identify an impact hypothesis that can be tested.
3. Build the submitted work within the event window; keep a disclosure record for outside resources and AI.
4. Validate the core functionality or make the mockup realistically testable.
5. Prepare public review links, a clear written pitch, and a demo of at most two minutes.
6. Save a complete submission before **4 October, 21:00 AEDT**; leave upload time.
7. Keep review links live and check later announcements for voting, ceremony time, and unresolved submission details.
